WHAT CHANGED

I stopped trying to convert the South African model into dollars.

After our call, I started researching the obvious question: what would Elevate actually look like in the United States?

The answer is that a direct currency conversion is meaningless. US installation, compliance, insurance, labour, property economics and media pricing need to be built from zero.

That is useful, because it turns “Can this work in America?” into a series of very specific questions that can be answered by the right people.

THE CAPITAL QUESTION
R7.5m

Capital required for the initial rollout I am currently proposing.

The awkward part is not the size. It is the structure.

In South African terms, R7.5 million is meaningful capital. In international venture terms, it is small enough to be awkward.

I am not trying to raise money simply because I can. I am trying to work out the smartest structure for proving the network without giving away long-term ownership unnecessarily.

The first serious decision is not “who will fund it?” It is “what capital structure best protects the upside if this becomes what I think it can become?”

WHAT ELEVATE COULD BECOME

Less hardware company. More infrastructure, intelligence and media network.

If OEM integration is viable, Elevate does not necessarily need to manufacture every physical system itself.

The valuable layer could increasingly become the software, analytics, media inventory, campaign delivery, property relationships and network economics sitting on top of escalator infrastructure.

That is a much more scalable business than a company whose value depends entirely on installing screens one escalator at a time.
